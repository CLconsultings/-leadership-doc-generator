# Output modes: detailed guidance

Load this file when a task needs more than the one-line shape in `SKILL.md`. Each mode
has a required structure and a set of failure modes to avoid. The reasoning posture and
voice rules in `SKILL.md` apply to every mode.

## Executive explanation

Purpose: help a decision-maker act. Write in this order.

1. The real tension. Name what is actually in conflict beneath the presenting issue.
2. Why it matters. Tie the tension to a concrete cost, risk, or opportunity.
3. Behavioral and operational implications. What this changes in how people work.
4. The decision or action required. Be specific enough to act on.
5. The risk of getting it wrong. What happens if the decision is delayed or wrong.

Avoid: burying the decision, listing options without a recommendation, and softening
the risk into vague caution.

## Coaching response

Purpose: build the person's judgment while moving them toward action. Follow the
sequence.

1. Clarify the situation. Confirm what is actually happening.
2. Identify the underlying tension.
3. Separate assumptions from observable evidence.
4. Help the person think before you give advice. Ask, reframe, or surface a
   distinction they are missing.
5. Provide one practical next action.
6. Include exact language when it helps them do the hard part.

Do not solve the problem too quickly. If you hand over the answer before the person has
thought, you have produced advice, not coaching, and the learning is lost. One good
next action beats a list of five.

## Workshop or facilitator language

Purpose: give a facilitator words that work when spoken aloud in a room. Keep sentences
short and speakable. Include:

- the purpose of the segment
- the participant behavior being developed
- the practical application
- the transition or facilitator prompt that moves the group forward

Avoid promotional language. Facilitator copy is an instrument for a live session, not
marketing. If a line would sound like a brochure when read to a room, rewrite it.

## Public writing

Purpose: express Eileen's judgment to an outside audience without exposing anything
proprietary or confidential.

Protect internal frameworks, client examples, internal corrections, and unapproved
program details. When a point would require confidential material to make, generalize
it or make a different point. The reasoning can be fully present in public work; the
proprietary scaffolding and client specifics cannot.

## Private strategic analysis

Purpose: give Eileen or an internal stakeholder the sharpest possible read of a
decision.

Go deeper into behavioral and systems analysis than the other modes. Challenge
assumptions, including the framing of the request, where doing so improves the
decision. Label uncertainty and evidence gaps explicitly so the reader can weight them.
This is the one mode where directly naming what is unproven or unknown is a feature, not
a hedge.
